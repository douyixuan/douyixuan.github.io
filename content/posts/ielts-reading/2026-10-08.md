## Today's article

**“Discovering the value of humanistic inquiry”** — Elizabeth Durant, *MIT News*, October 7, 2026.

## Introduction

MIT's Concourse combines scientific study with humanistic inquiry. Its students examine difficult questions through reading and discussion. The article invites IELTS readers to consider how education develops judgment alongside technical knowledge.

## Vocabulary

1. **zeitgeist** — the characteristic outlook of an era.
2. **polarizing** — dividing people into opposing groups.
3. **humanistic** — concerned with human experience and values.
4. **thorny** — difficult to resolve.
5. **aggregation** — a collection formed by combining items.
6. **springboard** — a starting point for further development.
7. **cultivate** — develop through sustained attention.
8. **foster** — encourage growth or development.
9. **rigorous** — careful and logically thorough.
10. **quantitative** — expressed or investigated through numbers.
11. **judgment** — the ability to evaluate and decide thoughtfully.
12. **ethos** — the guiding character or values of a group.
13. **siloed** — separated from other groups or areas of knowledge.

## Reading comprehension

**1. Why does Linda Rabieh believe that the rise of artificial intelligence makes humanistic education more important?**

{{< answer >}}
AI makes questions about humanity and intelligence more urgent, inviting students to examine whether intelligence exceeds calculation and information gathering.
{{< /answer >}}

**2. What distinguishes MIT's Concourse program from a traditional engineering education?**

{{< answer >}}
It integrates humanities with mathematics and science, using classic texts and debate to explore human existence.
{{< /answer >}}

**3. Why do some students initially avoid expressing opinions about political or philosophical questions?**

{{< answer >}}
Some assume that without definitive answers they have little to contribute; others lack a method for examining such issues.
{{< /answer >}}

**4. How does the article distinguish quantitative reasoning from the judgment required for moral and political decisions?**

{{< answer >}}
Numbers cannot settle every policy or ethical question. Careful non-quantitative reasoning can still support considered judgments.
{{< /answer >}}

**5. What broader argument does the article make about the purpose of university education?**

{{< answer >}}
Education should develop independent thinkers and responsible citizens as well as capable scientists and engineers.
{{< /answer >}}

## Expression of the day

IELTS Writing adaptation: Education can strengthen both technical competence and independent judgment.
