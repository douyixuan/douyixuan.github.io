## Original article

**The Guardian:** https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/aug/11/ai-will-do-more-to-boost-fossil-fuel-production-than-green-energy

## Introduction

This article examines a policy tension: AI can improve renewable-energy systems, but it can also increase the productivity of oil and gas extraction. The study models 64 scenarios and argues that indirect effects across the energy system may matter more than focusing only on AI data-center electricity consumption.

## Vocabulary

**outweigh** — be greater in importance or effect; **productivity gain** — increase in output or efficiency; **fossil fuel** — coal, oil, or natural gas; **renewable energy** — energy from replenishable sources; **carbon emissions** — release of carbon dioxide; **optimize** — make more efficient; **deployment** — practical implementation; **at scale** — at large operational size; **pilot project** — small preliminary implementation; **adoption** — taking up a technology; **outpace** — grow faster than; **break even** — reach a balance; **speculative** — based partly on uncertain assumptions; **unchecked** — not adequately controlled.

## Reading comprehension

1. Why can AI increase emissions even if it improves renewable-energy efficiency?
2. Why distinguish between pilot projects and deployment at scale?
3. What does the study's “four times” threshold mean?
4. Why might focusing only on data-center electricity use underestimate AI's climate impact?

<details><summary><strong>Reference answers — open after you finish</strong></summary>

1. AI can simultaneously make fossil-fuel exploration and production more productive, potentially increasing fossil-energy output enough to outweigh efficiency gains in renewables.
2. A promising pilot does not necessarily have a significant system-wide effect; mature large-scale deployment changes actual production and emissions much more.
3. In the modeled scenarios, AI-driven productivity improvement in renewables needs to be roughly four times that in fossil fuels for the emissions effects to balance.
4. Data-center consumption captures AI's direct energy use but misses the much larger indirect effects created when AI changes productivity and investment across energy industries.

</details>
